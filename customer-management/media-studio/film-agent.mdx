## What the Film Agent is

The **Film Agent** is an AI director built directly into the Generative Media Studio. It appears as a chat panel (the "dock") on the right side of the storyboard canvas. Customers describe what they want, and the agent:

1. Conducts a brief interview to establish genre, mood, and story beats
2. Creates a project, storyboard, character sheets, and environment plates
3. Proposes a shot-by-shot plan for each scene with an upfront credit quote
4. Renders each approved shot and assembles the scenes into a finished film

Every charged step (generation, frame extraction, assembly) requires the customer to approve the quote before credits are deducted. The agent cannot exceed the thread credit ceiling set at connection time.

## Prerequisites

- The **Generative Media Studio** must be enabled for the customer (see [Generative Media Studio overview](/customer-management/media-studio/overview)).
- The customer must have enough credits to cover at least one scene. The Film Agent shows a running cost ledger and stops at the ceiling.
- A Studio token with the Film tool grant (40-tool grant) must be active. Partners issue Studio tokens from **Partner Portal → AI Agents → MCP Tokens**.

## Starting a film session

1. Open the **Generative Media Studio** from the customer portal sidebar.
2. Click **New Film** or open an existing storyboard and click **Film Agent** in the top-right toolbar.
3. The Film Agent dock opens on the right. Start by describing the film you want to make.

## The production workflow

### Step 1 — Brief and story beats

The agent asks a series of short questions about:
- Genre and visual style
- Characters and locations
- Scene structure (how many scenes, approximate length)

Answers become the **story bible** and **beat sheet**, both saved to the storyboard.

### Step 2 — Character and environment assets

Before shooting, the agent creates:
- **Character sheets** — four-panel reference images at ¾-body crop
- **Environment plates** — wide-angle establishing images for each location

These become **Elements** on the storyboard (see [Cinema mode](/customer-management/media-studio/cinema-mode)).

> 📷 **Screenshot needed:** _Add a screenshot of the Elements panel in the storyboard showing character-sheet and environment-plate thumbnails organised by category._

### Step 3 — The 480p gate

For each scene, the agent renders a low-resolution **480p test clip** first. This costs roughly 46 % of the full-resolution price. The customer approves or rejects the gate before the 720p render starts.

If the gate is skipped (available at an additional credit cost), the 720p shot runs directly.

### Step 4 — 720p production shots

Approved shots are rendered at **720p**. The storyboard canvas shows each shot as a take card in the **film rail** (a horizontal strip at the bottom of the Canvas view).

### Step 5 — Assembly

When all shots are approved the agent calls **Assemble** to splice scenes together using ffmpeg. Assembled films keep their audio (audio leg per shot or a silent bed merged with cross-fade). The finished film is saved to the customer's **Media Library** and linked on the storyboard.

## Credit spend cap

Each Film Agent session has a **thread credit ceiling**. The ceiling is set when the Studio token is minted (default: 6,000 credits). The agent tracks spend in real time and stops accepting new charges when the ceiling is reached.

Spend counting:
- Blocked renders are refunded and do not count toward the ceiling
- The ceiling resets per thread (not per session)

Partners can review per-thread spend in **Partner Portal → Credits → AI Credits** and filter by customer.

## Supported film lengths

The Film Agent produces films by stitching scenes. Scene lengths depend on the quality tier:

| Quality tier | Scene length |
|---|---|
| Everyday | Up to 5 s per shot |
| Studio / Best | Up to 15 s per shot |
| Long-form | Up to 30 s per shot |

Multi-scene films assemble into longer pieces. *The Paper Boat*, a 61-second, three-scene film, was produced by the agent in a live proof run.

## Exporting the finished film

Finished films are accessible via a signed URL from the media-exports bucket. The signed URL is the only way to read the file (direct public access returns 403). URLs do not expire by default — the day-prefix TTL sweep keeps the bucket clean.

From the storyboard, click **Export** → **Download film** to get the signed URL. You can also share it directly from the export panel.
